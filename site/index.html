Ask for a dashboard, and it builds you one

One request, and the assistant lays the whole export out as a page of your own: what needs attention, every result against its lab's range, your care history, your medicines. Claude named this one HealthOS.

One button, and it collects your records

Maccabi Online shows your medical records one page at a time, and only so far back. The Chrome extension goes through every section for you.

  1. Log in to Maccabi Onlineas usual, in Chrome.
  2. Press Start exportClick the extension's icon in Chrome's toolbar: it's under the puzzle piece until you pin it. Maccabi texts you, because this orders a fresh copy of your full medical file.
  3. Leave the tab openUsually a few minutes, up to 20. The ZIP lands in your Downloads.

Free. It installs in Chrome on a computer.

Your records and their PDFs, in one folder that's yours

Your full medical file on top, then a folder for each part of Maccabi Online: lab and test results, visits, prescriptions, referrals, vaccinations, letters.

Take it to a private doctor or one abroad. Keep it if you move or switch health funds. It's yours forever.

Not included: imaging studies, and visit summaries older than 12 months, which the site doesn't keep. See every folder

Why not drop it into a chat?

A chat takes a few dozen files at most. Your export has hundreds, and the assistant finds its way through them with the folder's own layout and README.md. The desktop apps read the folder as it is.

Built so you can check every answer

The export's README asks the assistant to:

  • Show its source

    The file, the page of a PDF and the date behind each fact, so you can open it and look.

  • Leave your export alone

    Its notes, and what you tell it, go in a folder of its own: point it to that folder when you export again. It's asked never to edit, rename or delete your files.

  • Keep you out of searches

    It's asked to keep your name, ID number and contact details out of web searches and other tools unless you ask.

  • Say when it matters

    It's asked to explain rather than diagnose, and to say plainly when a record suggests seeing a doctor soon.

Questions about exporting your Maccabi medical records

Is this an official Maccabi tool?

No. It is unofficial: not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by Maccabi Healthcare Services. It is open source, and it works in the session you are already logged in with. It never sees your password or your one-time codes.

Is it free?

The extension is free. The assistant is its own product: Claude needs a paid plan to open a folder, and ChatGPT opens one on its free plan where Work has rolled out.

Where do my medical records go?

From Maccabi Online to your own computer, as one ZIP file. The extension talks only to Maccabi Online and has no server of its own, so nothing is sent to its developer. An AI assistant reads them only if you open the folder in one.

Does it change anything in my Maccabi account?

One thing: each export orders a fresh copy of your full medical file, so Maccabi texts you as it starts. Everything else only reads.

How far back do the records go?

The site's own form orders the medical file from July 2020 at the earliest. The export asks for your whole history, the widest range Maccabi allows. Each other section holds what Maccabi Online has in it.

Does it work on a phone, or in another browser?

It installs in Chrome on a computer, version 120 or newer. There is no phone version.

Does it work with Clalit, Meuhedet or Leumit?

No. It reads Maccabi Online only.

My records are in Hebrew. Can I ask in English?

Yes. The assistant reads the Hebrew and answers in plain English, and it names the record behind each answer, so you can check it.

The service you open them in can read them. Check what it keeps and for how long, and where it lets you:

  • Give each person a project of their own, and check what its memory shares with your other chats.
  • Delete the chats and the project when you're done with them.
